App themes

Pick how Heed looks, make your own theme, or have an AI assistant design one. This page is for you and for the AI.

What a theme changes

A theme sets the colours, the font (one font for all the text), the text size, how round the corners are and how thick the outlines are, across every screen of the app. The alarm screen follows your theme too: it uses the theme's danger colour, flashes with its background (if screen flash is on), and uses its heading font. Its text is white or black, whichever is easier to read. Only the status card on the main screen and the countdown keep their own amber and red in every theme, so those signals are always unmistakable.

Open the menu (top left) and tap App theme. Tap a theme to use it. Turn on the Match the phone switch to use Daylight while your phone is in light mode.

Make your own

  • Create your own theme starts from the theme you're using. Change any colour, the font, the text size, corners and outlines, with a live preview.
  • Built-in themes never change. Duplicate one (or one of yours) to get a copy under Your themes that you can edit.
  • Your themes are listed under Your themes, where you can edit, duplicate or delete them.
  • If a colour makes text hard to read, the editor says so and offers the nearest colour that is readable (it follows the WCAG contrast guidelines).
  • Your themes are in the app's backup file (menu, Backup and restore), with their widget looks, so they move to a new phone with the rest.

Widget look

Each theme also sets how the Heed home screen widget looks. Open the menu, tap App theme, edit one of your themes and open the Widget tab. You can set:

  • Font, text size, and bold, italic or underline
  • Background colour and its opacity (down to see-through)
  • Text, secondary text and accent colours
  • Border colour and width, and corner roundness

Anything you leave unset follows the theme, so a theme that never touches the widget draws it in its own colours and font. By default the widget sits on the theme's cards colour (surface). The widget keeps its look while the alarm rings.

Make one with AI

  1. On the App theme screen, tap AI prompt.
  2. Describe the theme you want, like "warm sunset colours, rounded corners, a techy font, bigger text", and tap Copy full prompt.
  3. Paste it into any AI chat (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude...). The prompt tells the AI what Heed is, where this page is, the format and your current theme.
  4. Copy the AI's whole answer, then tap Paste the answer and Import. The new theme is saved with your themes and switched on.

Nothing is sent anywhere by Heed: you copy and paste yourself, to the assistant you choose.

Import a theme

Tap Import a theme, then paste a theme's JSON or choose a file. An AI's whole answer is fine (the JSON can sit in a code block among other text), and so is a theme someone shared. A backup file works too. Only themes are imported: anything else in the file is left alone, and text with no theme in it changes nothing.

Format reference

A theme is one JSON object. Everything is optional: whatever is left out comes from the theme you're using, so a theme can be as small as one colour.

{
  "name": "Sunset",
  "dark": true,
  "background": "#1A1210",
  "surface": "#241A16",
  "surfaceVariant": "#30221C",
  "border": "#4A332A",
  "accent": "#FF8A3D",
  "onAccent": "#1A0E06",
  "text": "#FBEFE8",
  "mutedText": "#C9AFA3",
  "danger": "#FF5C5C",
  "font": "Chakra Petch",
  "textScalePct": 105,
  "cornerDp": 20,
  "borderDp": 1
}
FieldTypeWhat it does
nametextThe theme's name, up to 40 characters.
darktrue / falseWhether it's a dark theme (status bar icons follow it). Left out, it's worked out from the background.
backgroundcolourThe screen behind everything.
surfacecolourCards and sheets.
surfaceVariantcolourInputs, chips and tonal buttons.
bordercolourOutlines and dividers.
accentcolourButtons, switches, links and highlights.
onAccentcolourText and icons on accent buttons.
textcolourMain text.
mutedTextcolourSecondary text, hints and icons.
dangercolourWarnings and errors, and the alarm screen.
secondarycolourOptional. Secondary buttons and selected chips, with text on them. Left out, the accent tinted into the cards. Also worked out again when a theme changes accent, surface, background, text or dark without naming it.
fonttextOne font for all the text: Phone default, Condensed, Serif, Mono, Audiowide, Oxanium, Orbitron, Michroma, Tektur, Rajdhani, Chakra Petch, Share Tech Mono. Case and spaces don't matter.
textScalePctnumberText size, 85 to 130 (percent). It multiplies your phone's own text size.
cornerDpnumberCorner roundness, 0 (square) to 28.
borderDpnumberOutline width, 0 to 3.

Widget

A theme can also have a "widget" object for the home screen widget. Every key is optional. Leave a key out, or write null, and it follows the theme.

"widget": {
  "background": "#10202A",
  "opacityPct": 85,
  "accent": "#FF8A3D",
  "borderDp": 0,
  "cornerDp": 24
}
FieldTypeWhat it does
fonttextThe widget's font, from the same list as the theme's. Follows the theme's font.
textScalePctnumberText size, 85 to 130 (percent). Follows the theme's.
bold, italic, underlinetrue / falseApplies to every text on the widget. Left out, each text is drawn the way Heed draws it.
backgroundcolourThe widget's colour. Left out, it's the theme's surface.
opacityPctnumberHow solid the background is, 0 (see-through) to 100. Left out, 100 (Clumsy Clanker: 75).
text, mutedText, accentcolourMain text, secondary text, and the title and highlights. Follow the theme's.
bordercolourOutline colour. Follows the theme's.
borderDpnumberOutline width, 0 to 3. Follows the theme's.
cornerDpnumberCorner roundness, 0 to 28. Follows the theme's.

Colours in the widget are made fully opaque; use opacityPct for see-through. Keep text at least 4.5:1 against the widget's background, and mutedText and accent at least 3:1.

Colours are "#RRGGBB", like "#FF8A3D". Numbers outside their range are brought into it.

Readable text: aim for a contrast of at least 4.5:1 for text on background and on surface, for onAccent on accent, and for text on secondary when you set it; at least 3:1 for mutedText on surface. A theme that misses this is still imported, with a note saying what is hard to read, and the editor can fix it.

What the importer forgives: the JSON can be inside a code block or among other text. A value it can't read (a colour written as "red", a font it doesn't have) keeps the previous one, with a note, and the rest still imports. headingFont and bodyFont from older versions are read as font.

More shapes

Several themes at once, choosing which one to use by its name:

{"custom": [{"name": "Sunset", "accent": "#FF8A3D"}, {"name": "Forest", "accent": "#3DDC84"}], "selectedId": "Forest"}

Just switch to a built-in theme (graphite, daylight, neon, clumsy-clanker, high-contrast):

{"theme": "clumsy-clanker"}

A small change to the theme you're using, here a red accent:

{"name": "Red accent", "accent": "#FF5252", "onAccent": "#1A0000"}

An old app-wide "widget" setting (outside a theme) is ignored, with a note: the widget look now lives inside each theme.

A theme can also be wrapped as {"theme": {...}}, the way it appears in a backup.
